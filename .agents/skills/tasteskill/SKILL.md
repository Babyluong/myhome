# Page purpose and visual judgment

This local adaptation uses TasteSkill's brief-inference idea. The active product system and user intent take precedence over generic aesthetic defaults.

## Brief first

Identify audience, page purpose, primary action, current tokens and approved references. Infer settled details from the task/workspace; ask only when a consequential choice remains unresolved.

For operational dashboards and workflows, favor predictable structure, legible dense data, restrained motion and discoverable actions. For login/hub identity surfaces, allow a deliberate hero, larger display typography, meaningful whitespace and the approved dimensional composition. Do not classify every view as a dashboard.

## Design choices

- Choose density, visual variation and motion for the actual task. Operational density and low-motion recommendations do not impose a typography ceiling or reject an approved login hero.
- Reuse the current spacing/type/color/elevation tokens. Scale headings and form regions for ordinary laptops, narrow screens and wide displays; verify actual geometry and zoom rather than relying on a fixed pixel list.
- Build hierarchy around the primary action. Use comparable numerical formatting for tables and metrics; avoid invented statistics, badges or testimonials.
- Adapt layout to content and behavior. Master/detail, panels and whitespace are options, not mandatory templates.
- Coordinate light/dark materials and backgrounds, then review rendered evidence against the brief. A tasteful source file is not proof of a satisfying screen.
